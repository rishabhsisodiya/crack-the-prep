Part of the [system design](/prep/system-design) series. This is a **delta page** on top of [Video Streaming (YouTube)](/prep/system-design/video-streaming-youtube): upload/transcode pipelines, CDN delivery and adaptive bitrate all carry over. This page covers what's specific to music.

## 1. Requirements

**Functional**
- Search and browse a catalog of ~100M tracks; play instantly; skip, seek, queue.
- Playlists (user-created, collaborative, editorial), library, follows.
- Offline downloads for premium users.
- Personalized recommendations and generated playlists.
- Accurate play counts — they drive **royalty payments** to rights holders.

**Non-functional**
- **Time to first sound** matters most: well under a second. Users skip constantly, so the next track must start immediately.
- ~600M+ users, tens of millions concurrent streams; huge long tail (most tracks rarely played, a few played billions of times).
- Play events must be durable and auditable (money depends on them).

## 2. What's the same as video

- Ingest from labels/distributors → transcode into several bitrates/codecs (e.g. Ogg Vorbis/AAC at ~96–320 kbps) → object storage → CDN.
- Catalog metadata in a database, search via an inverted index (see [Search Engine](/prep/system-design/search-engine)).
- Signed, expiring URLs for media.

## 3. What's different

**1. Files are small, so fetch the whole thing.** A 3-minute song at 160 kbps is ~3.5 MB. Instead of segmenting like video, clients fetch in a few large range requests, start playing after the first chunk, and **prefetch the next track in the queue** while the current one plays. Skip latency then disappears. Clients also keep an on-disk cache of recently and frequently played tracks.

**2. Long-tail distribution.** Popular tracks are always hot in CDN edges; long-tail tracks miss the CDN and come from origin. Tiered caching (edge → regional → origin) keeps long-tail misses cheap.

**3. Offline and DRM.** Downloaded tracks are stored encrypted on the device; keys are tied to the account and device, and the app must check in periodically (e.g. every 30 days) to keep offline content valid. Device download limits enforce licensing terms.

**4. Play counts for royalties.** A "stream" is usually counted only after a minimum listen (commonly 30 seconds). Clients send play events, buffered locally when offline and uploaded later with original timestamps. The pipeline must be **exactly-once in effect**: event ids for dedup, raw events stored durably, and batch jobs producing the authoritative counts. Fraud detection (bot farms streaming tracks) runs on these events before payouts. See [Distributed Counter](/prep/system-design/distributed-counter) for the fast, approximate public counts.

**5. Playlists.** Some playlists are followed by millions (editorial ones) — read-heavy, cache aggressively. Collaborative playlists need concurrent edits: store ordered items with fractional/positional keys, or as an append-only change log, so two people adding tracks don't overwrite each other.

**6. Recommendations.** Built offline from listening history: collaborative filtering (users who play X also play Y), audio/content embeddings for new tracks without history, and NLP on playlist titles. Generated playlists (Discover Weekly-style) are computed in batch and served as ordinary playlists.

<Callout type="tip">
The two music-specific points interviewers want: **prefetch the next track** (instant skip) and **play events are financial records** (durable, deduplicated, auditable, fraud-checked). Everything else, say "same as video" and move on.
</Callout>

## 4. What real systems do today

- **Spotify** has written extensively about its event delivery system (moving to Google Cloud Pub/Sub and Dataflow), its use of **Cassandra** for user data like playlists, and recommendation approaches combining collaborative filtering with audio and text signals.
- Spotify's public guidance is that a stream counts toward royalties after 30 seconds of playback.

## Interview follow-ups

- *"How do you make skipping instant?"* — Prefetch the next queued track; local cache; small files fetched whole.
- *"How do you count plays for royalties?"* — Minimum-listen threshold, durable deduplicated event pipeline, batch authoritative counts, fraud filtering.
- *"User is offline for a week."* — Encrypted local files with an offline license; play events queued on device and uploaded later.
- *"A playlist with 30M followers."* — Read-mostly, cache and CDN its contents; edits are rare and invalidate the cache.
